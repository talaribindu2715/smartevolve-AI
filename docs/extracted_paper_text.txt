Abstract—Learning platforms that update a student’s profi-
ciency after every attempt can be gamed by repeating the same
assessment, and the credentials they issue are hard to verify
independently. This paper studies one narrow design choice
and its surrounding system. SmartEvolve AI updates proficiency
with a learning rate that decreases with the attempt count,
orders learning resources with a prerequisite graph, scores career
readiness as a weighted fulfillment ratio, and anchors validated
achievements on an Ethereum Virtual Machine (EVM) ledger as
a pseudonymous student hash and an achievement digest, so that
no cleartext personal data is written on-chain. In a Monte Carlo
simulation with 10 seeds of 2,000 simulated learners each, the
damped update reduced the score inflation caused by ten retries
of the same assessment from 38.9 to 23.8 points compared with
a constant-rate update. The cost is slower tracking of genuine
improvement (mean absolute error 11.9 versus 6.8 points), and a
running-mean update was competitive on stationary skills. The
benefit is therefore a trade-off, not a universal gain. The study
uses simulated learners and synthetic workloads, and no user
study was performed.

Index Terms—adaptive learning, proficiency estimation, pre-
requisite graph, career readiness, blockchain credentials, smart
contracts

I. INTRODUCTION

Learning management systems usually present a fixed se-
quence of courses, whatever the learner’s starting point or
pace. Platforms that do adapt must decide how much a new
assessment score should change the stored estimate of a skill.
If the estimate moves too slowly, genuine progress is ignored.
If it moves too quickly, a learner can raise it by retaking the
same quiz until the answers are memorized. Credentials add
a second problem: certificates sit in separate databases, so a
third party cannot tell whether a record has been altered.

Prior work addresses parts of this picture. Cognitive tutors
and knowledge tracing model what a learner knows [1], [2].
Prerequisite graphs order content [3]. Blockchain has been
proposed for educational records [6], [7]. These are usually
studied separately.

This paper asks a narrow question. If a platform updates
proficiency after every attempt, how much of the inflation from
repeated retries can be limited by damping the learning rate
with the attempt count, and what does the damping cost when
a learner genuinely improves? We study it inside a working
architecture, SmartEvolve AI. Our contributions are as follows.
• An attempt-damped proficiency update with a stated
bound: the estimate always moves toward the score and
never past it.

• A simulation study of three scenarios (stationary skill,
genuine improvement, repeated retries), two baseline up-
date rules, ten seeds, and 95% intervals.

• A prerequisite-graph sequencer with cycle detection, and
an explainable career-readiness measure illustrated with
a worked example.

• A dual-hash credential anchoring scheme, together with

an account of what it does not guarantee.

II. BACKGROUND AND RELATED WORK
Adaptive learning and knowledge tracing. Cognitive tu-
tors established model-based adaptation of instruction [1].
Bayesian Knowledge Tracing and Deep Knowledge Tracing
[2] estimate mastery from response sequences. Neural models
can be accurate but are hard to interpret. Lightweight paramet-
ric rules with confidence terms are easier to audit and cheap
to run, which motivates the update studied here.

Prerequisite sequencing. Prerequisites form a directed
acyclic graph (DAG), and a valid study order is a topological
ordering [3], [4]. Adaptive hypermedia systems have used such
structures to guide learners [5].

Blockchain credentials. Blockchain has been proposed for
educational records and credentials [6]–[8], and Ethereum
smart contracts [9] allow a public registry without a central
verifier. Placing identifying data on a public ledger raises
privacy concerns [10], so designs that store only commitments
are preferred.

VI. SECURITY, DISCUSSION AND LIMITATIONS

Tamper evidence. Once a digest is on-chain, altering the
off-chain record changes its hash and verification fails, so a
compromised server cannot silently modify an issued creden-
tial. Verification shows that a record is unchanged; it does not
show that the issuer was legitimate, which is why issuance is
restricted to authorized accounts.

Data minimization. Only a salted student hash and an
achievement digest are stored on-chain; names, e-mail ad-
dresses, and transcripts stay in the access-controlled database,
and quiz answers are evaluated on the server. Pseudonymous
hashes may still count as personal data under regulations such
as GDPR, and immutability conflicts with erasure rights, so the
design reduces rather than removes compliance obligations.
Deleting the off-chain salt and record makes the on-chain
digest unlinkable in practice.

What the evidence supports. Damping the learning rate with
the attempt count limits the score inflation caused by repeated
retries, by about 39% against a constant rate in our simulation.
It does so at a clear cost: genuine improvement is tracked
more slowly, and a plain running mean is a strong alternative
on stationary skills and nearly as good against retries at the
tenth attempt. The result supports damping where gaming is
the main concern and assessments are noisy. It does not show
that the proposed form is optimal.

Limitations. First, all learners are simulated, and the score
model, noise levels, and memorization rate are assumptions.
Second, the difficulty weights and the square-root form were
not fitted to real data. Third, the sequencing and gas bench-
marks use synthetic workloads and a test network, and the gas
values must be re-measured. Fourth, the career benchmarks
are a controlled set of roles that need periodic review. Fifth,

no pilot study with students was run, so effects on learning
outcomes are unknown.

Practical use. The update is a constant-time computation
per attempt, so it can run inside the backend without extra
infrastructure. A natural next step is to fit the weights on pilot
data and to compare against a Bayesian Knowledge Tracing
baseline.

VII. CONCLUSION

We studied an attempt-damped proficiency update inside
the SmartEvolve AI architecture, which also provides pre-
requisite sequencing, career-readiness scoring, and hashed on-
chain credentials. In simulation, damping reduced retry-driven
inflation by roughly 39% relative to a constant rate but tracked
genuine improvement less closely, so the choice depends on
how much gaming matters. Future work includes a pilot study
with real learners, fitting the model parameters, comparison
with knowledge-tracing baselines, and zero-knowledge proofs
for threshold claims such as grades.
